# CRM Capability & Integration Map

## Executive summary

Mapped the capabilities of a white-label CRM and automation platform to evaluate how it could complement an internal software product. The work organized a broad feature set into business capability domains, identified potential integration points, and created a framework for deciding what to adopt now, integrate, defer, or build internally later.

## Business question

How can the company gain mature customer-management and communication capabilities quickly while preserving a long-term internal product vision?

## Capability map

| Domain | Representative capabilities | Strategic relevance |
|---|---|---|
| CRM | Contacts, opportunities, pipelines, task tracking | Shared customer and sales record |
| Communication | SMS, email, phone, conversation inbox | Centralized customer engagement |
| Automation | Triggers, workflows, reminders, routing | Lower manual workload and faster follow-up |
| Marketing | Forms, landing pages, campaigns, reputation tools | Lead generation and nurturing |
| Scheduling | Calendars, appointments, confirmations | Reduces booking friction and no-shows |
| Reporting | Funnel, campaign, attribution, activity reporting | Management visibility |
| Platform | APIs, webhooks, permissions, white labeling | Integration and branded customer experience |

## Decision framework

- **Adopt:** valuable standard capability available with minimal customization
- **Integrate:** capability should exchange data with the internal platform
- **Configure:** business rules can be achieved through native workflows
- **Build later:** strategically differentiating capability that should remain proprietary
- **Avoid/validate:** weak fit, excessive dependency, data risk, or unclear ROI

## Key evaluation criteria

Business fit · User experience · API coverage · Data ownership · Security · Scalability · White-label limits · Total cost · Migration risk · Vendor dependency
